**Source:** `partnerships/the-juice-lagos-cocktail-week.html` (rendered locally, commit `bebbc42`) and its homepage card
**Target Level:** Not specified, reviewed against Senior (read as a senior brand and partnerships role; the rubric is built for product design, so Interaction Design is excluded)
**Review Mode:** Online Portfolio
**Overall Score:** 46/100 (41 of 90 applicable points; Interaction Design N/A)
**Evidence Coverage:** Medium
**Hiring Readiness:** Needs Significant Work

**Could not inspect:** the linked Instagram reel (Instagram is unreachable from the review environment). The page shows a still frame from it, which was inspected.

**First impression:** A reader understands in seconds that Oluwatoni led a newsletter's booth at Lagos Cocktail Week and that Instagram followers nearly doubled; they do not yet see what the booth did, why it was designed that way, or what Pulse wanted from it.

## 1. Executive verdict

This is the strongest of the four partnership case studies because it has a clear role ("Led the partnership activation"), a baseline and after value, and an honest qualifier on the growth figure. It is held back by what is missing between the role and the result: the objective, the reasons behind the booth concept, what visitors actually did there, and any reflection. Today it reads as a strong summary card rather than a case study. Fix first: add the booth mechanics (the photo on your homepage already shows a prize wheel with rewards like "Feature Post" and "Shout-Out") and explain why that mechanic turns visitors into followers.

## 3. What works

1. **A clear, honest metric**: a baseline, an after value and an explicit qualifier make the growth figure credible rather than inflated.
2. **A memorable idea**: "Taking a digital lifestyle community beyond the screen" tells the reader the strategy in one sentence.
3. **Clear ownership**: the role is personal and consistent across the homepage card and the case page.

## 4. What isn't working

### Severity: High: The booth concept is never shown or explained
- **Evidence:** "An interactive branded booth at Cocktail Village." (Execution and outcome)
- **Why it matters:** the decision that shows your judgment is reduced to six words, so a reader cannot see how you think.
- **Fix:** describe the booth mechanics and why they were built to convert visitors into followers; add the booth photo.

### Severity: High: No objective or partner value
- **Evidence:** the page has no goal statement.
- **Why it matters:** a partnerships lead is hired on commercial judgment; without an objective the result cannot be judged against anything.
- **Fix:** state what Pulse wanted, what Lagos Cocktail Week got, and how success was defined.

### Severity: Medium: Metric missing period and source
- **Evidence:** "Instagram grew from ~300 to 565 followers during the activation period, +88.3%."
- **Why it matters:** an interviewer will ask when and how this was measured.
- **Fix:** add dates and the data source.

### Severity: Medium: Bullet lists instead of a story
- **Evidence:** three bullet blocks under The context, My contribution, Execution and outcome.
- **Why it matters:** readers skim bullets as tasks, not decisions.
- **Fix:** short paragraphs that link the insight to the booth design to the result.

### Severity: Low: No reflection
- **Evidence:** not present.
- **Why it matters:** missing a chance to show judgment and growth.
- **Fix:** one honest lesson.

## 5. Storytelling audit

- **Opening hook:** strong; the subtitle states the idea clearly.
- **Narrative structure:** the chain breaks between Insight and Decision: we learn the context, then that a booth existed, but not why it was built that way.
- **Clarity:** high; every line is easy to read.
- **Pacing:** very short; the decisions get less space than the context.
- **Decision rationale:** missing.
- **Outcome presentation:** good, with the qualifier placed next to the number.

**Remove:** "What it shows" line; let the story show it.
**Move:** the booth photo from the homepage into this page, directly after the context.
**Condense:** the three contribution bullets into one sentence about your role.
**Expand:** the booth mechanics and why each element existed.

## 6. Visual evidence audit

- **Supports the story:** the reel still proves the venue and the Pulse and The Juice branding at the entrance.
- **Missing:** the booth itself, the prize wheel in use, any Instagram post generated by a "Feature Post" or "Shout-Out" reward, and a simple follower chart.
- **Needs annotation or redoing:** the reel still is a small, low-resolution frame (203 x 360 source); a higher-resolution frame or photo would read better at this size.
- **Interactions to demonstrate:** a visitor spinning the wheel and the reward appearing on Instagram.
- **Understandable from images and captions alone?** Partly: the venue is clear, the concept is not.

## 9. Before and after

### Execution and outcome
**Original:** "An interactive branded booth at Cocktail Village."
**Problem:** states that a booth existed, not how it worked or why.
**Improved:** "We built The Juice's booth around a prize wheel. Visitors could win a Shout-Out, a Feature Post, Free Juice, a sticker or a hand fan, so the rewards pulled people from the booth onto The Juice's Instagram. [ADD: why you chose these rewards and how visitors claimed them]"
**Missing evidence:** how rewards were claimed and how many visitors played.

### My contribution
**Original:** "Worked across planning, brand experience and execution."
**Problem:** generic task list.
**Improved:** "I led the activation end to end: [ADD: the two or three calls you made, for example the booth concept, the reward mechanic, the partner agreement]."
**Missing evidence:** the specific decisions you owned.
