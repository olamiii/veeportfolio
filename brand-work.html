Coca-Cola turned its bottle into chopsticks

This might be one of the smartest marketing campaigns I've seen this year.

In parts of Asia where chopsticks are a key part of dining culture, Coca-Cola transformed its iconic contour bottle into a pair of chopsticks.

Simple.

But powerful.

A lot of brands spend time asking:

"How do we get more attention?"

The better question is:

"How do we become part of people's lives?"

The most successful brands don't just advertise.

They find ways to embed themselves into existing habits, behaviours, and cultural moments.

Because when a brand naturally fits into what people are already doing, it stops feeling like marketing.

It starts feeling relevant.

Marketing isn't always about being louder.

Sometimes it's about becoming part of the experience.

KFC rebranded around the bucket

How do you modernize a 74-year-old brand without erasing its history?

KFC may have found the answer.

The hardest part of rebranding isn't changing how your brand looks.
It's changing how your brand feels without losing what made people love it in the first place.

KFC launched a full global rebrand on Monday.
And according to JKR, the agency behind the work, the entire system was built around KFC's most iconic asset:

The bucket.

Not the Colonel.
Not the recipe.

The bucket.

Millions of people have a feeling attached to that red and white cardboard.

A birthday.
A late-night craving.
A family meal.

KFC didn't invent those moments.
But over time, they became part of them.

The new identity, developed with agency JKR, turns the bucket into a full visual system, a framing device across packaging, restaurants, digital, and social.

Everything anchors back to the one thing people already have an emotional response to.

Most brands rebrand by adding.
New logos.
New colours.
New stories.

KFC went the other direction.

They looked at the thing people already remembered...
and built everything around it.
The bucket stopped being packaging.

It became the brand.

Most rebrands chase a new narrative.
This one doubled down on what people already loved.
